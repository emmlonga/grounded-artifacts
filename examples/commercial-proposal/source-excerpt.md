# What the sources looked like

A reconstructed summary, not quotations. The original sources were two governing documents: a commercial document and an implementation plan several times its length. A third annex was explicitly excluded. Together they ran to tens of thousands of words. Below is what each contributed and where the two pulled apart: the places the grounding had to settle before any prose was written.

## What each source governed

| Concern | Commercial document | Implementation plan |
|---|---|---|
| What is sold | An owned capability, framed for the buyer | The workflow: selected documents → prepared interpretation with evidence → specialist review and confirmation → calculation and report for supported cases |
| Scope | "Complete product" | Complete only within a defined profile of supported calculation structures, frozen in the first weeks; out-of-profile cases are classified, never approximated |
| Ownership | The client owns the application | Components (AI models, reference tables, possibly the calculation engine) are reused; the engine is a replaceable layer chosen during implementation |
| Delivery | Three stages, approximate milestones, a price per stage, payment terms | Acceptance principles for stages 1 and 2; the end of stage 3 is not yet defined |
| Support | A monthly allowance of scheduled hours in stage 3; defect fixes within the accepted scope do not use it; no new features | — |
| Running costs | Recurring third-party costs fall to the client after transfer; development and pilot usage included up to reasonable use; caps still open | Which third-party services the product depends on (cloud, AI models, possibly an external calculation service) |
| Value | Less manual work | The gain has no measured baseline yet: measure it, do not promise a figure |

## Where they pulled apart

- **"Complete" and "bounded".** Read alone, the commercial framing invites "supports all these calculations". The plan does not support that. Resolution: complete for a defined initial scope, which is frozen early and extended only by validation.
- **"Owned" and "reused".** "The client owns it" could be read as "owns every component". Resolution: the client controls the product and its evolution, and market components are reused where that is cheaper and reliable.
- **Support hours and defects.** One sentence about support could easily blur the two. Resolution: a must-not-say row, plus wording that keeps defect fixes outside the allowance.
- **Evolution and learning.** "The product improves with use" sounds like automatic training, which the plan rules out. Resolution: improvements are prioritized by what real use shows.
- **What was still open.** Acceptance mechanics, the end of stage 3, the choice of calculation engine, running-cost caps and maintenance after rollout were open. Each became an open item with the venue where it would close, so the proposal could say "set in the definitive proposal" instead of inventing an answer.
