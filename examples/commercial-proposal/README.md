# Example: an alignment-stage commercial proposal

This example is derived from a real workflow run. Identifying details, commercial terms and case-specific facts have been generalized or reconstructed for public use. The workflow behavior and editorial problems illustrated are representative of the original run.

**The situation.** A B2B technology firm (the proposer) prepares an alignment-stage proposal for an enterprise buyer. Today the buyer's specialists read case documents and prepare financial calculations by hand. The proposal must let a commercially sophisticated, non-technical decision-maker check direction, scope, timing and price before anyone invests in a definitive proposal. The decision-maker may forward it to legal and IT reviewers.

**The difficulty.** The sources were a commercial document and a much longer implementation plan. Each was generous in a different place: "complete" and "owned" in the commercial framing, bounded scope and replaceable components in the plan. The proposal had to stay short and commercially concrete without promising more than the plan supports.

## Conventions

- Bracketed values (`[Client]`, `[Proposer]`, `[TOTAL]`, `[PRICE-1]`, `[A]`, `[N]` …) stand in for parties and commercial constants that are not published. In a real run they come from the grounding's constants table, in their accepted renderings.
- The original was written in another language for a specific industry. This version is newly written in English and describes the domain generically. It is not a translation of the original document.
- The private records are not included: grounding versions, audit files, adjudication history and source documents. This folder shows what the workflow did, not the record of it.
