Story frame template. Copy to <project>/story-frame.md once the factual grounding (pass A) is audited.
The frame records the editorial selection: what this artifact is about, which claims carry it, and what stays out.
It is a hypothesis about the story, never a source, and it points rather than restates: a beat, a decision or an
outcome line names what happens and cites the pass-A rows; the claim text, qualifiers, hedges and open-item caveats
stay in the rows it cites, and the frame carries none of them. A line may not imply more than its rows hold. The frame does not reproduce source qualifiers verbatim, but its
shorthand must stay semantically faithful to any qualifier that changes the meaning of the proposition (an aim is not
a result; "agreed to report" is not "agreed"). If the grounding later weakens a cited claim, the frame
changes, never the fact. Nothing in the frame repeats another section of the frame. The outline's governing thought is
this frame's thesis. Size: about 250–400 words for a three-page artifact is the design intent, not a limit; a frame
that reads like the grounding in sentences has stopped being a decision record.
Current truth only: reasons for changes live in audits/frame-adjudication.md.
-->
