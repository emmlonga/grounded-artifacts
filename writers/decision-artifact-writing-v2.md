# Decision Artifact Writing v2

## What this skill is for

Preserve the evidence and reasoning the outline requires, at the force the grounding gives it, and reduce the effort this reader needs to take it in. Shortness is never the goal: a cut needs a nameable redundancy. The skill governs expression and local development only.

It does not own: what may be claimed (the grounding); what is included, excluded, sequenced or asserted as a takeaway (the outline); what a decision must carry for this genre (the mode's writing rules and the outline's handoff rules); whether the reader reaches the objective (the Audience auditor); whether meaning survived (the Integrity auditor); layout (the profile); the author's personal style (out of scope).

## Precedence and authority

Brief, then grounding (claims, constants in accepted renderings, anonymization map, §6 audience terms, editorial rules), then outline (Include, Exclude, takeaways, closed decisions, caps), then the mode's writing rules, then this skill. Where a content rule says what a sentence must carry, this skill decides only how.

- The grounding is the only source material. No claim, number, example, experience or causal link enters from anywhere else. A gap is a decision for owner in the return, never a marker in the text.
- Every Include item is present in its section; every Exclude is respected; the takeaway is the section's destination and is never rendered or paraphrased as a sentence; section placement never changes. Within a section, sentences and paragraphs may be reordered unless the outline states a local order. The outline's headings stand unless the delegation says Format lines and heading wording are advisory.
- The section caps and the planning total govern the first draft. One fit pass, only when the preview shows unused capacity, with section caps soft and the guardrail and page maximum binding; fit edits are readability-only and are listed in the return.
- Never recover a grounding row the outline left out because it would make the story stronger. If the outline prevents a good artifact, say so in the return; do not work around it.

## Set the dials from the brief and the mode

Before drafting, read the brief's audience, objective, evaluation criteria and constraints and the mode's writing rules, and set from them: directness, warmth, personality, density, evidence and polish; person, register and tense. This skill carries no artifact's values. A memo or report reader wants the decision before the reasons; a story reader wants events in the order they happened; a reader who will probe wants each decision's cost where it can be found. Where the mode says the structure doubles as a talk track, headings and labels that carry the argument are content, not scaffolding.

## While drafting: four questions in play

**What does this reader already hold, and what am I adding?** Open a sentence from what the reader has and end it on what is new. One idea per sentence; split any sentence the reader would have to backtrack to parse; keep a long sentence when one visible subject carries it. Define a term at or before its first use, in the grounding's §6 audience words. If §6 has no rendering, use a plainer expression only when it carries exactly the grounded meaning; otherwise keep the grounded term and flag the missing audience rendering in the return. Never invent a definition. If the outline excludes a gloss, keep the term and make the sentence readable without it. On a jump in time or topic, restate the context in one clause.

**Why does this follow?** Each sentence and each paragraph follows the previous one for a reason the reader could name, or the transition names it; connectors only where the order fails. Signposting test: keep a device that reduces the reader's navigation effort and delivers what it announces (a count followed by its items; a labelled cost, alternative or hindsight marker the mode says the reader probes; a heading that carries the argument where the mode calls the structure a talk track). Remove a device that repeats visible structure, announces what never arrives, labels nothing, or stacks (ordinals piled across a piece). Act on a habit across the piece, never on one sentence.

**Who did what, and what happened?** Actions are verbs with actors and consequences are events. Systems may do what the evidence attributes to them; do not give an inanimate thing human judgment. When a process noun hides who acted or whether the event occurred, unpack it; keep the noun when it is the clearest name for the thing. Prefer actor, object and evidence for a factual claim, only when the grounding names the mechanism: "The loader parses the file", not "the file is parsed"; "The compiler rejects a renamed column at build time", not "type safety is improved". When the grounding is vague, keep it vague.

**Does this say exactly as much as the grounding does?** An aim is not an achievement; an emphasis is not a cause; an estimate is stated as an estimate and only a confirmed fact is stated as fact. Scope and ranking words and qualifiers stay attached to their claims; a canonical term is not varied for variety. Make the reasoning the outline requires legible: the actor and ownership, the rationale, the rejected alternative and the consequence, where the grounding holds them and this reader needs them. Whether the reader meets each as a labelled slot or as an event is your call under the signposting test.

## After the whole draft: one self-review, then stop

- Does every sentence add information this reader did not already have, or orientation, relation or emphasis that reduces this reader's effort? If neither, cut or merge it.
- Preservation, by the completion guard: every Include present; owned versus influenced as the grounding's ownership row; hindsight marked as the grounding marks it; every constant in an accepted rendering and every §6 term as given; scope and ranking words in place; the last sentence of each section read against the grounding; nothing stated that the grounding does not state or directly imply.
- Structure: the backtrack test; uniform cadence (three consecutive sentences of the same length are the place to look); the reshuffle test (if paragraphs could trade places, fix the order, not the connectives); proportion (an emphasised sentence in every paragraph is a habit); every announced count delivered; the relation test at paragraph seams (can the reader name why this paragraph follows; if the word cannot be supplied without inventing a relation, there is none); whether a skimming reader would get each paragraph's message from its opening. These are checks; none is a rule about shape.
- Read once at speaking pace, in the register the brief and mode require, with the reader in the brief in mind: stumbles, breathless clauses, lecturing, a sentence that exists only to feel complete, repeated shape.
- Per-sentence pass: mark each sentence plus, minus or zero; act only on the minuses.

Then stop. Do not repolish sentences you already judged sound. One review per call.

## Fit pass

Only when the preview render shows unused capacity, as the runbook defines. Moves: split a sentence at the second inference; move a gloss or qualifier into its own sentence beside its claim; name a relation the order left implicit; restore a dropped actor, article or verb. Never a fact, example, number or claim, and never a change of force. List every edit in the return.

## Revising after audit findings

Light-edit mode. Every word outside the changed regions starts as kept; a change needs a failure you can name; a hit on any pattern is not a failure. Check auditor wording against the grounding before using it. Apply the four questions to the changed sentences and their immediate neighbours, then read the whole affected section at speaking pace for coherence, still editing only the changed regions and their neighbours. A defect noticed elsewhere goes into the return as an observation, not into the file. Put back any change you cannot justify. One self-review of the changed regions, then stop.

## Never

- Upgrade "we" to "I" or dilute "I" to "we"; add or strengthen a causal link; change the factual chronology or the semantic attachment of an event, duration, aim or actor (prose order within a section stays editable as above).
- Restate an aim, hope or plan as achieved, or an emphasis as a cause; drop a scope or ranking word; remove a qualifier or move it away from its claim.
- Add an example, number, detail or next step the grounding does not hold; render a constant outside its accepted renderings; vary a canonical term for variety.
- Convert hindsight into rationale at the time; flatten an unusual concrete detail into a general statement.
- Remove a heading, label or count the mode marks as reader-facing on the strength of a pattern alone; shorten as a goal.
- Edit outside the changed regions in revise mode; use the fit pass for content.

A single feature is never a defect; a habit across the piece is the signal.
